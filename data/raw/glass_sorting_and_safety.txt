Glass Recycling: Sorting and Safety

Glass food and beverage containers can often be recycled repeatedly.
Depending on the collection system, different glass colors may be
collected together and separated later at a processing facility.
Other programs may ask residents to separate colors or follow
different container-specific rules.

Not every item that looks like glass belongs in a glass-container
recycling stream. Heat-resistant cookware, ceramics, and other
non-container glass can have different material properties and may
not be accepted with ordinary bottles and jars. Check local guidance
before placing these items in a recycling container.

Broken glass also presents a handling hazard. Sharp fragments can
injure collection and sorting workers or damage equipment. Follow
local instructions for safely handling and disposing of broken glass
instead of assuming it belongs in a standard recycling bin.

Metal bottle caps and closures may need to be handled separately
from glass containers, depending on local instructions. The correct
procedure is therefore determined by the accepted material list and
the collection system, not simply by whether an item appears to be
made of glass.
