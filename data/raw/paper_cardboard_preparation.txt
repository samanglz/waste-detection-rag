Preparing Paper and Cardboard for Recycling

Paper and cardboard are commonly collected for recycling, but
preparation can affect whether a particular item is suitable. Flatten
cardboard boxes when the local program requests it, and remove loose
food scraps from paper food containers. Food residue and other
contaminants can reduce the quality of recovered paper or complicate
processing.

Not every paper product is treated in the same way. Shredded paper,
laminated or glossy wrapping paper, books with hard covers, and
food-service packaging may have specific preparation or acceptance
rules. Some programs accept materials that others exclude, so local
instructions take priority over general advice.

Reuse can be preferable when an item is still useful. For example,
a book in good condition may be donated instead of immediately
recycled. Reusable gift bags and boxes can also reduce the amount
of material entering the waste stream.

Paper recycling typically involves mixing recovered paper with water
to produce pulp. Processors may remove unwanted materials before
the pulp is used to manufacture new paper products. Keeping collected
paper suitable for the intended process helps protect the quality
of the recovered material.
