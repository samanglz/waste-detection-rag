Plastic Resin Codes and Actual Recyclability

A resin identification code on a plastic container identifies the
type of plastic resin used to make that item. Examples include PET,
often used for beverage bottles, and HDPE, commonly used for some
detergent and milk containers. The code can help identify a material,
but it is not a guarantee that the item is accepted by a local
recycling collection program.

Actual recyclability depends on several factors: whether the local
program collects that resin, whether suitable sorting and processing
facilities exist, and whether the material can be recovered in a
usable form. Different products made from plastic may therefore have
different disposal options even when their appearance is similar.

Plastic bags, wraps, and films can require a separate collection route
rather than placement in a household recycling cart. Some retail
locations accept these materials, but acceptance and preparation
requirements should be checked before drop-off.

Compostable plastics should not automatically be mixed with
conventional recyclable plastics. They may require a dedicated
composting service and can disrupt a conventional plastic recycling
stream when incorrectly mixed. Always follow the rules published by
the relevant local recycling provider.
