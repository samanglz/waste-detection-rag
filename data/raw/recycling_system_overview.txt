Recycling System: Collection, Processing, and Remanufacturing

A recycling system commonly involves three broad stages: collection,
processing, and remanufacturing. During collection, households and
businesses place eligible materials into curbside programs, drop-off
locations, or other collection systems. The available options depend
on the local waste-management infrastructure.

At a processing facility, collected materials are sorted by material
type and prepared for further processing. Contaminants may need to be
removed because unsuitable materials can interfere with equipment
or reduce the quality of recovered commodities. Different materials
require different preparation methods. For example, recovered glass
may be processed at a glass-processing facility, while plastics may
be sent to a specialized plastics reclaimer.

In the remanufacturing stage, processed materials become inputs for
new products. Paper mills and glass or metal manufacturers are examples
of facilities that can use recovered materials. Successful recycling
therefore depends not only on collection but also on sorting quality,
processing capacity, and demand for recovered materials.

Recycling rules and infrastructure vary by locality. A material's
potential to be recycled does not guarantee that every community
collects or processes it.
